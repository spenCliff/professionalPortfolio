Formula Student Wind Tunnel Testing and CFD Validation

Wind Tunnel Formula Student Experimental Correlation

Led CFD validation against a 50% scale Formula Student wind-tunnel campaign, combining force measurements, pressure tappings and flow visualisation to assess and improve simulation fidelity.

Overview

As Head of Computational Fluid Dynamics for Southampton University Formula Student, I supported the development and validation of the team's aerodynamic simulation methodology against experimental wind-tunnel data.

A 50% scale vehicle was tested across a range of operating conditions using force-balance measurements, surface pressure tappings and flow visualisation. The resulting data was used to assess the accuracy of the CFD model and identify areas for further development.

What I Did

Worked across both the experimental and computational sides of the campaign, establishing comparable conditions between the wind tunnel and CFD simulations.

Wind-Tunnel Testing

  • Supported the setup and operation of a 50% scale vehicle in the wind tunnel.
  • Collected aerodynamic force data alongside surface pressure measurements and flow visualisation.
  • Controlled test conditions to provide repeatable data for CFD comparison.

CFD Methodology

  • Developed and refined the STAR-CCM+ vehicle model using mesh and domain convergence studies.
  • Compared turbulence models to assess their influence on predicted aerodynamic forces and flow structures.
  • Matched CFD boundary conditions and vehicle configuration to the experimental test case.

Flow Analysis

  • Used pressure measurements and flow visualisation to investigate key aerodynamic features and regions of flow separation.
  • Compared experimental observations against CFD pressure and velocity fields to assess whether the simulation reproduced the underlying flow behaviour.

Correlation

The CFD model was correlated against experimental force and pressure measurements under matched conditions. Rather than assessing the simulation solely through numerical convergence, the comparison provided an independent check of its physical accuracy.

Aerodynamic Force Correlation

  • Compared CFD predictions against wind-tunnel balance measurements across the tested operating conditions.
  • Achieved force-coefficient agreement within 1% between simulation and experiment.
  • Used the correlation to assess the suitability of the CFD methodology for future aerodynamic development.

Pressure & Flow Correlation

Surface pressure measurements and flow visualisation provided additional insight into the sources of agreement and disagreement between CFD and experiment, helping distinguish modelling limitations from measurement uncertainty.

Results & Reflections

The validation campaign demonstrated that the developed CFD methodology could reproduce the vehicle's measured aerodynamic forces to within 1%. This provided greater confidence in using the simulation to guide aerodynamic development and design decisions.

The project strengthened my understanding of the relationship between CFD assumptions, experimental measurement and aerodynamic performance, while providing experience working across the full simulation-to-test validation process.
