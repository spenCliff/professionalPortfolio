Formula Student Wind Tunnel Testing and CFD Validation

Wind Tunnel Formula Student Experimental Correlation

Our 50% scale vehicle was tested across a range of operating conditions using force-balance measurements, surface pressure tappings, and flow visualisation to assess the accuracy of our CFD setup.

Overview

As part of the Southampton University Formula Student Team, I supported the setup and operations of our wind tunnel testing. Data from this experiment was subsequently used in a validation study of the team's CFD setup.

Methodology

I worked across both the experimental and computational sides of the campaign, establishing comparable conditions between the wind tunnel and CFD simulations.

Wind-Tunnel Testing

A total of 24 pressure tappings were distributed across the aerodynamic components for static pressure readings.

The spine was attached directly to the moment and force balance which allowed for easy measurements and control of yaw and rake angles.

As well as the balance and pressure readings, we used tufts to visualise vortex shedding on the rear wing and fluorescent 'flow-vis' paint to identify flow patterns and mimic line integral convolution oil flow diagrams.

A rolling road and boundary layer suction device were used to accurately simulate real boundary layer conditions.

Rotating wheels were attached to individual load cells to measure their drag contribution.

CFD Setup

The CFD boundary conditions and turbulence parameters were carefully calibrated to replicate the physical environment of the R.J. Mitchell wind tunnel.

The computational geometry was scaled to match the 50% physical model. From there, an iterative meshing approach was used to resolve local sensitivities, systematically reducing discrepancies with the experimental data to improve overall correlation.

Correlation

The CFD model was correlated against experimental force and pressure measurements under matched conditions. Rather than assessing the simulation solely through numerical convergence, the comparison provided an independent check of its physical accuracy.

Results & Reflections

The validation campaign demonstrated that the developed CFD methodology could reproduce the vehicle's measured aerodynamic forces to within 1%. This provided greater confidence in using the simulation to guide aerodynamic development and design decisions.

The project strengthened my understanding of the relationship between CFD assumptions, experimental measurement and aerodynamic performance, while providing experience working across the full simulation-to-test validation process.
