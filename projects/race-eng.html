Distance-Aligned Lap Comparison & Driving-State Analysis

FastF1 Python Race Engineering

A telemetry pipeline that aligns two F1 laps onto a common distance axis, tracks the cumulative time delta between them metre by metre, and auto-generates plain-English coaching notes on exactly where and how one driver gained — later braking, more corner speed, or an earlier return to throttle.

Overview & Analytical Framework

Every number in this pipeline comes straight from FastF1 telemetry or a simple threshold applied to it — there's no clustering, no fitted model, and no assumed car parameters (mass, downforce, tyre friction). Raw speed, throttle, brake and position channels are resampled onto a uniform 10 Hz time grid and smoothed with a Savitzky–Golay filter before differentiating, which keeps the acceleration signals from blowing up on near-duplicate telemetry timestamps.

1. Longitudinal acceleration: $a_{long} = \dfrac{d}{dt}\,v_{smooth}(t)$

2. Path curvature (from smoothed x, y): $\kappa = \dfrac{x'y'' - y'x''}{(x'^2 + y'^2)^{1.5}}$

3. Lateral acceleration: $a_{lat} = v^2 \kappa$

State priority: Brake % > 5 → Braking; elif |a_lat| > 0.3g → Cornering; elif Throttle % > 50 → Accelerating; else Coasting

The priority ordering matters: trail-braking into a corner still reads as Braking even though lateral g is already climbing, because brake pressure is checked first. Both accelerations are clipped at ±8g as a backstop against residual differentiation noise, not because that limit reflects anything physical about the car.

Methodology & Implementation

The pipeline pulls two laps via FastF1, builds a kinematic working dataframe for each, then aligns them on distance to read off exactly where time was won or lost.

# Classify each sample by driving state - fixed thresholds, no ML
def classify_state(mp):
    braking = mp['brake'] > BRAKE_THRESHOLD
    cornering = (~braking) & (mp['latAcc'].abs() > LAT_ACC_THRESHOLD)
    accelerating = (~braking) & (~cornering) & (mp['throttle'] > THROTTLE_THRESHOLD)

    state = pd.Series('Coasting', index=mp.index)
    state[accelerating] = 'Accelerating'
    state[cornering] = 'Cornering'
    state[braking] = 'Braking'
    return state

Driving-State Classification

  • Every 10 Hz sample is tagged Braking / Cornering / Accelerating / Coasting purely from cut-offs on brake %, lateral g, and throttle %, checked in that priority order.
  • No fitting, no clustering — just thresholds on channels that are either raw telemetry or a direct kinematic derivative of it.

Per-Driver State Track Maps

  • Each classified lap is drawn back onto the circuit as a continuous, colour-segmented line, so the state sequence reads as a single trace rather than a scatter of gapped points at high speed.
  • On a fast circuit like Silverstone the map comes out mostly blue and green, with red braking confined to just a handful of corners — a sanity check that the thresholds are doing something sensible before trusting the delta analysis built on top of them.

Distance Alignment & Cumulative Delta

  • Both laps' time-vs-distance curves are interpolated onto a common 5 m grid; the cumulative delta at each point is simply timeB − timeA, so the trace shows exactly who is ahead, and by how much, at every metre of the lap.
  • A Savitzky–Golay-smoothed gradient of that delta gives the local rate of gain or loss, which is then grouped into contiguous same-sign stretches (minimum 15 m) and ranked by time gained — the raw material for the section-by-section report.

Automated Coaching Notes

For the largest time-gain segments, the script pulls the handful of directly observable facts within that stretch — each driver's minimum (apex) speed, where braking starts, where full throttle resumes — and turns "X was faster here" into something a driver can actually act on. A console report lists the top sections in order, each with the underlying differences that cleared a minimum threshold (±3 km/h corner speed, ±3 m on brake or throttle points), and falls back to a "smoother, more consistent line" note when no single measurement stands out.

BOT +0.xxx s   dist xxxx–xxxx m  (during Cornering)

→ HAM: try to carry more corner speed; brake later; get to full throttle sooner (like BOT did here).

Every clause in that sentence is conditional on real telemetry evidence for that exact stretch — nothing is templated in if the numbers don't support it.

Results & Future Development

Run on Hamilton vs Bottas' 2019 British GP Q3 laps, the cumulative delta trace shows Hamilton opening a lead of over three tenths within the first kilometre out of Turn 1, before Bottas clawed almost all of it back across the long middle sector, pulling to within a tenth by half-distance. Hamilton edged back ahead in the closing sector for a wafer-thin margin at the flag — the kind of see-saw pattern a single lap-time gap completely hides, and exactly what the distance-aligned view is built to expose.

Future work: extending the state classifier with named-corner annotations so the coaching notes read "into Village" rather than a raw distance range; averaging the segment analysis across a full stint instead of a single lap pair to separate one-off scruffy corners from a driver's consistent weak points; and an interactive HTML version (Plotly) with hover tooltips linking each delta-plot feature straight to its telemetry trace.

Placed side by side, the two drivers' state maps line up almost corner-for-corner — expected for team-mates on the same car — which makes the handful of places they diverge (a slightly earlier brake point, a touch more coasting into one corner) exactly where the cumulative delta trace above shows time actually changing hands.
