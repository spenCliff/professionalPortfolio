Telemetry-Based Vehicle Performance Mapping

Vehicle Dynamics Python Race Engineering

Extracting dynamic downforce coefficients ($C_L$) and mapping performance regimes using single-lap race telemetry, friction-limit modeling, and spatial trajectory integration.

Overview & Mathematical Framework

This project evaluates dynamic vehicle limits by processing multi-channel telemetry metrics including speed, longitudinal acceleration, lateral acceleration, throttle position, and brake pressure. By modeling total normal force ($N = mg + L$) relative to tire friction boundaries ($\mu = 1.8$), we isolate pure aerodynamic downforce contributions from static mass effects.

1. Vectorized Acceleration Magnitude: $a_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2}$

2. Normal Force Balance: $F_{\text{max}} = \mu N = \mu \left(m g + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 S C_L\right)$

3. Limit Balance Equivalence: $m a_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{max}}$

Governing $C_L$ Equation: $C_L = \frac{2m}{\rho S V^2} \left( \frac{\sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2}}{\mu} - g \right)$

Data points are filtered across power-saturated straightaways and transient shifting events to guarantee downforce estimation ($C_L$) occurs strictly when the chassis operates at the tire traction limit.

Methodology & Implementation

The Python analytical pipeline processes raw lap telemetry to segment dynamic performance envelopes, derive lift coefficients, and quantify trajectory deltas.

Multi-Channel Telemetry Ingestion

  • Processed time-series channels across a 5,000m lap, synchronizing vehicle speed, $a_x$, $a_y$, throttle position, brake pressure, steering angle, and DRS actuation.
  • Applied signal filtering to eliminate transient sensor noise and shifting artifacts prior to calculating dynamic force vectors.
# Calculate dynamic aerodynamic lift coefficient (CL)
mp['CL'] = (
    ((2 * carMass) / (rho * frontArea * mp['speed']**2)) * 
    ((np.sqrt(mp['longAcc']**2 + mp['latAcc']**2) / mu) - g)
)

# Isolate true grip-limited peak downforce
maxCL = mp['CL'].max()
print(f"Maximum downforce coefficient: {maxCL:.2f}")

Aerodynamic Limit Isolation

  • Vectorized NumPy transformations evaluate dynamic pressure ($q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$) and vehicle load balance at scale.
  • Isolated maximum downforce values strictly within traction-bound corners and braking zones.

GG Friction Circle Expansion

  • Mapped combined lateral and longitudinal g-forces against the static friction boundary ($\mu = 1.8$). High-speed braking zones reach up to ~4–5g, directly demonstrating aero downforce scaling.
  • Separated telemetry points into distinct domains: Power-Limited (engine output bounds acceleration) and Grip-Limited (tire traction bounds performance).

Power vs. Speed Envelope & Spatial Tracking

  • Evaluated engine saturation thresholds across vehicle velocity ranges to confirm straightaway power limits versus cornering grip limits.
  • Reconstructed full spatial circuit geometry (using integrated telemetry velocity vectors) to compare dead-reckoned track trajectories against GPS coordinates.

Results & Future Development

The aerodynamic limit model effectively separates engine power constraints from tire traction limits, showing clear GG circle expansion beyond the static $\mu = 1.8$ threshold under high-speed dynamic loads. Reconstructing spatial circuit coordinates from velocity telemetry revealed localized positional drift during trail-braking entry phases.

Future work involves adapting the pipeline to ingest live FastF1 data feeds, adding suspension potentiometer channels for pitch/roll dynamic downforce mapping, and implementing a Kalman Filter to eliminate telemetry integration drift against real-time GPS coordinates.
