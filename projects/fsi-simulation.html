Fluid Structure Interaction of a Formula One Front Wing

FSI STAR-CCM+ CFD Aeroelasticity Transient Simulation

Modelled transient FSI behaviour of a front wing to characterise structural deflection under varying aerodynamic loads across a straight-line acceleration/deceleration cycle.

Overview

The aim of this project was to investigate the aeroelastic behaviour of a front wing during an acceleration and deceleration cycle.

Telemetry data from Kimi Antonelli’s pole lap at Silverstone was used to reconstruct an approximate vehicle velocity profile along the Hangar Straight, providing the transient aerodynamic loading for the simulation.

What I Did

The simplified structural material model and isolated aerodynamic geometry mean the results are intended to demonstrate aeroelastic behaviour and FSI methodology rather than predict the deformation of a specific physical Formula 1 front wing.

The following assumptions were therefore adopted:

  • Incompressible flow,
  • k–ω SST RANS turbulence model,
  • Linear elastic structural behaviour,
  • Simplified shell-based structural representation,
  • Isolated front wing geometry,
  • Moving ground plane.

Two-way coupling was used, allowing structural deformation to modify the fluid domain and subsequent aerodynamic loading.

Simulation Setup

  • Mesh refinement.
  • Shell-based structural model.
  • Two-way FSI coupling

Transient Loading

  • Transient velocity profile.
  • Moving ground plane.

Preconditioning

  • The wing was preconditioned using a constant inlet velocity of 6.9 m/s for 4.5 seconds, corresponding to approximately 4.5 fluid-through times.
  • This initialisation period allowed the structural response to settle before the transient velocity profile was applied. Rayleigh damping was also introduced to dissipate residual structural oscillations and improve the stability of the coupled FSI solution.
