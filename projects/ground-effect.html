Ground-Effect Aerodynamics of a Fan Car

Aerodynamics CFD Ground Effect STAR-CCM+

Investigated the aerodynamic behaviour of a ground-effect fan car, focusing on ride-height sensitivity, underbody flow physics and correlation against experimental data.

Overview

Ground-effect vehicles generate downforce by accelerating airflow beneath the floor and creating a region of low pressure. However, this performance is highly sensitive to ride height, with excessive proximity to the ground potentially causing flow separation and a loss of downforce.

I developed a CFD model of a simplified fan car to investigate these effects, examining the relationship between ride height, underbody flow and aerodynamic performance before correlating the results against experimental data.

What I Did

Built a validated CFD model before using it to investigate the sensitivity of ground-effect performance to ride height and underbody geometry.

Geometry & CFD Setup

  • Developed a simplified ground-effect vehicle geometry capturing the key aerodynamic features of the underbody.
  • Created the computational domain and ground boundary to reproduce the relevant test conditions.
  • Applied local mesh refinement and near-wall treatment around the floor, diffuser and wake.

Ground-Effect Investigation

  • Simulated a range of ride heights to quantify their effect on downforce and drag.
  • Analysed pressure distributions, velocity fields and flow structures beneath the vehicle to identify the mechanisms driving performance changes.

Flow Physics

  • Investigated the acceleration of underbody flow and subsequent pressure recovery through the diffuser.
  • Examined the transition from increasing downforce to performance deterioration at low ride heights.

Correlation

CFD predictions were compared against published experimental data at matched operating conditions to assess whether the model reproduced the observed aerodynamic behaviour.

CFD vs Experiment

  • Compared aerodynamic coefficients across the available ride-height conditions.
  • Evaluated both the magnitude and trend of the predicted aerodynamic forces.
  • Used percentage deviation to quantify correlation and identify regions of greater modelling sensitivity.

Correlation Results

The model reproduced the overall aerodynamic trends with ride height, providing confidence in the underlying ground-effect behaviour. Differences between CFD and experiment were considered in the context of geometric simplification, turbulence modelling and the sensitivity of the underbody flow.

Results & Reflections

The study demonstrated the strong sensitivity of ground-effect performance to ride height. Increasing downforce was not simply a case of reducing ground clearance; beyond an optimum region, changes in the underbody flow caused aerodynamic performance to deteriorate.

Correlation highlighted the importance of validating CFD methodology before using simulation results to guide aerodynamic design decisions. The project strengthened my understanding of ground-effect flow physics and the relationship between aerodynamic performance and vehicle ride height.
